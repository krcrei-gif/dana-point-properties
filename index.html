Just a data point

The quarter in one paragraph

Dana Point is short on space and short on sellers. Retail vacancy sits at 2.3% and office is under 1%, yet only two retail buildings changed hands in the past twelve months and no office buildings did. Multifamily is where the activity is: six sales totaling $21 million, with vacancy down to 3.1% from roughly 5% a year ago.

Dana Point has been a market to watch for the last ten years. It has benefited from real momentum, a more favorable development climate than its neighbors, and big changes at the harbor and in the Lantern District.

Values have appreciated, but the current climate likely creates some unique opportunities for long-term property owners, with good returns compared with the complexity of Laguna Beach or the pricing of Newport Beach. It's a market that seems to be doing the right things to set itself up for success over the long run.

By district

Street by street

The Harbor

Dana Point Harbor is undergoing a major transformation through a public-private partnership with Dana Point Harbor Partners. The redevelopment includes new retail, restaurants, marina improvements, and two planned hotels. The hotels are currently stalled over a county lease vote.

For nearby property owners, the harbor's reinvention could reshape visitor activity, tenant demand, and investment expectations throughout Dana Point.

My perspective: The opportunity extends beyond the harbor itself. Watch how new hospitality and retail investment influences surrounding commercial properties.

Lantern District

The Lantern District is Dana Point's downtown commercial heart, centered along Del Prado Avenue and Pacific Coast Highway. Its mix of restaurants, boutiques, offices, and neighborhood businesses creates a distinctive, walkable coastal environment.

For property owners, the district offers opportunities tied to pedestrian activity, tenant mix, and the continued evolution of downtown.

My perspective: The most interesting question is how Dana Point balances its small-town character with growing demand for dining, retail, and hospitality.

Doheny Village

Doheny Village is one of Dana Point's most interesting areas for long-term reinvestment. Historically a mix of light industrial, service businesses, and commercial properties, the district has a different character from the city's coastal retail centers.

City planning initiatives have created a framework for revitalization, potentially opening the door to new uses and redevelopment opportunities.

My perspective: Watch the transition carefully. Zoning, infrastructure, parcel configuration, and development economics will determine which properties can realistically benefit.

Hospitality

Three hotel stories every owner should know

  • Ritz-Carlton, Laguna Niguel sells for $600 million KSL Capital Partners bought the Dana Point resort from Strategic Hotels & Resorts at about $1.6 million per room, the largest hotel sale in Orange County history. Orange County Business Journal, November 9, 2026
  • Laguna Cliffs Marriott becomes a JW Marriott A renovation of roughly $60 million will turn the 371-room hotel into The Ocean Cliffs at Dana Point, with completion targeted for early 2027. The owners chose to put Dana Point in the name. Orange County Business Journal, October 5, 2026
  • The two harbor hotels are stalled On August 25 the county Board of Supervisors split 2-2 on the lease change R.D. Olson needs to finance the $225 million, two-hotel project. The developer's fallback is renovating the existing Marina Inn. Orange County Business Journal, August 31, 2026

Something I'm seeing: capital is paying record prices for hospitality that already exists in Dana Point, while new rooms at the harbor remain hard to get built.

It is very difficult to develop today. That keeps capital focused on existing properties, which often trade below what it would cost to build new and offer a better risk-adjusted return.

Worth noting: The Wall Street Journal reported on October 5, 2026 that rising interest rates have buyers across the country asking sellers to renegotiate agreed prices before closing. Higher rates raise the bar for new development and for acquisitions alike.

In Dana Point, the numbers above answer the question of who has leverage. With retail vacancy at 2.3% and office under 1%, owners here face little pressure to concede on price.
